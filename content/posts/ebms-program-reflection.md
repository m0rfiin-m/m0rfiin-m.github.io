I entered the Entertainment Business master's program to develop my business skills and move toward a career connected to gaming, esports, or entertainment. Looking back at my completed work, I can point to business plans, marketing strategies, financial models, and presentations that show how I organize ideas and turn them into structured projects. My clearest takeaway is the ability to build a well-rounded deck, work within short deadlines, and adjust a project as requirements change. I am still working toward the career transition that motivated me to enroll. I see value in the work I produced, along with opportunities for the program to better connect coursework with professional practice.

My original Mastery Journey Framework identified specific goals for each course, including financial modeling, clearer communication, contract knowledge, and a defensible business plan (Morfin, n.d.). The reflections below compare those goals with the projects I completed. My earlier journal entries remain a record of how I viewed individual courses at the time. This final reflection describes where I stand now.

## Mastery Personal Development and Leadership

My original goal was to build stronger decision systems and leadership habits. The Mastery Journey Framework required me to identify industry trends, possible career paths, strengths, weaknesses, and the purpose of each course. I identified finance and hands-on negotiation as areas needing more development. That exercise gave me a written direction for the program. Looking back, the useful part is having a record against which I can compare my expectations and results. The framework gives me a way to assess progress through specific work rather than the degree alone. My next step is to connect those examples with the responsibilities of the roles I want to pursue.

## Executive Leadership

I wanted this course to improve my communication, influence, and ability to direct a team. My work included a personal branding strategy that connected my gaming background with the kind of work I wanted to pursue. I had to explain my audience, professional identity, and goals in a way another person could understand. That work addressed the communication side of my objective. The assignment gives me a practical starting point for explaining my professional direction. More opportunities to apply that strategy in team scenarios would help me connect the planning with day-to-day leadership.

## Business Storytelling and Brand Development

My goal was to strengthen pitch clarity and brand positioning. The brand canvases and creative strategy work required me to explain a brand's audience and direction. Working with esports examples made the assignments relevant to my interests. The practical issue was whether a reader could understand why the brand existed and who it was meant to reach. Those materials give me examples of how I organize a concept for an audience. The next step would be testing the message with its intended audience. That would help me connect the positioning work with feedback from people outside the classroom.

## Product and Artist Management

I expected to improve lifecycle planning and creator coordination. The Tempest Esports product-planning work focused on a desk mat and hoodie, including their target audiences, customer value, and proposed launch timing. The task required more than choosing merchandise I personally liked. Each product needed a reason to fit the concept and a plan for reaching its audience. This work addressed the planning portion of my objective and produced a concrete example for my portfolio. A useful next step would be comparing the proposal with supplier quotes and customer feedback before considering a launch.

## Entertainment Business Finance

My original objective was financial modeling and revenue analysis. The financial statement, time-value-of-money, break-even, and pro forma work required me to work through the numbers behind a business proposal. These assignments gave me financial documents to include in the larger planning process. I still see finance as an area where I need practice, especially when explaining and defending assumptions. More practice comparing forecasts with actual business results would help me build confidence in those decisions. The most useful application for me is checking whether the numbers support what a presentation promises.

## Digital Marketing

I wanted practice with marketing strategy and performance analysis across channels. I developed the Tempest Esports digital marketing strategy and final presentation, connecting a proposed gaming brand with its intended audience. The work required organizing a campaign rather than simply listing social platforms. It also provided material that could be condensed into a case study for an employer. The strategy demonstrates planning and audience analysis. A small live campaign would be a useful next step because it would let me compare the proposed approach with measured results.

## Negotiation and Deal Making

My goal was to become more comfortable with deal terms and negotiation. The coursework included negotiation worksheets, communication-style analysis, and an MLS television-deal case study. These activities required identifying positions, interests, alternatives, and the terms that mattered to each side. They gave me a structured way to explain a negotiation instead of describing it only as winning or losing. The work gave me practice analyzing a situation, organizing the relevant terms, and explaining a proposed approach. More live negotiation exercises with specific feedback would help me apply that structure under pressure.

## Advanced Entertainment Law

I expected to build practical familiarity with contracts, intellectual property, and risk. The intellectual-property audit, legal-liabilities presentation, and contract-review work required applying those topics to an entertainment business concept. The assignments made the proposed business more detailed because responsibilities and rights had to be stated clearly. For my portfolio, these are examples of research and issue identification. The practical value is identifying questions that need attention early in a project and recognizing when to involve a qualified legal professional.

## Entertainment Media Publishing and Distribution

My goal was to understand platforms, releases, and distribution strategy. The query-letter and self-publishing business-strategy assignments required planning how a work would reach an audience. My Built for the Feed project gave those decisions a specific subject and audience. My earlier journal entry discussed looking beyond the initial idea to the work involved in publishing it. That remains a useful description of the project. The resulting proposal shows how I considered audience, format, and distribution together. The project remains a publishing concept that could be developed further through audience feedback and a more detailed release plan.

## Project and Team Management

I wanted to improve reliable delivery and project scheduling. The ManaLink tournament plan connected a project definition with a work breakdown structure, budget, risks, and a comprehensive management plan. Those documents give me something concrete to show when discussing project coordination. The assignment required keeping the parts consistent as the plan developed. That fits the ability I recognize most clearly at the end of the program: completing structured projects within short time frames and adjusting them as needed. ManaLink remains a planned event. Executing a smaller version would be a useful way to test the schedule and risk plan against real conditions.

## Business Plan Development

The objective was an investor-ready business plan. I developed Tempest Esports through company and industry analysis, market and competition research, operations, management, and financial planning. My September journal focused on the gap between staffing costs and projected revenue. That issue required looking at whether the operating plan and financial assumptions supported each other. The course produced material for the final plan and required revisions across connected sections. I would describe the outcome as a developed academic business proposal. Further development would involve testing demand and defending the assumptions with evidence beyond the classroom.

## Final Project Business Plan

My original objective was a polished, defensible strategic plan. Bringing Tempest Esports into a final business plan and presentation is the clearest practical takeaway I can identify. I can create a well-rounded deck, complete projects within a short period, and make adjustments when requirements change. Those abilities are visible in the work I finished. The project brought the earlier coursework into one place and required the written plan and presentation to tell a consistent story. My next step is to use those materials to explain my contribution to employers. A stronger connection between the final project, industry feedback, and targeted job opportunities would make this transition more useful for students pursuing a career change.

## Next Steps

My next goal is to move into work connected to gaming, esports, or entertainment operations. I built my Hugo website myself and have updated the Tempest case study to explain the final project, my contribution, and the revisions made during planning. The portfolio gives me concrete examples to discuss with employers. I would like to see more structured opportunities within the program for live projects, feedback from industry professionals, and career guidance tied to students' target roles. Those additions could help students turn completed coursework into stronger evidence of professional readiness. For me, the next step is to keep refining the portfolio, seek outside feedback, and apply the project organization and presentation skills demonstrated throughout the program.
