Tempest Esports is a proposed FPS organization I developed through graduate coursework. My work at Tanbii shaped one priority: community operations should be part of the business plan from the beginning. For Tempest, that means clear moderation, recurring activities, and a process for turning player and fan feedback into decisions.

## The Starting Point

My competitive analysis considered how established organizations combine competition, content, and sponsorship. For Tempest, I chose a narrower starting point: an initial FPS roster supported by regular community activity. This is a proposed operating approach that still needs testing with players, fans, and sponsors.

The business would earn revenue through sponsorship packages and branded merchandise. Content would help fans follow the team and give sponsors a way to reach that audience. The plan keeps competition at the center of the organization.

## Community Operations in Practice

Players and fans would have clear ways to share feedback through community discussions and structured surveys. The team would review that feedback, identify what it could act on, and explain its decisions. Asking for input creates a responsibility to follow up.

Communication would cover what is happening with the roster, upcoming activities, and changes that affect members. When a plan changes, I would want members to understand what changed and what comes next.

The measurement plan would look beyond member count to recurring participation and engagement with team content. My experience at Tanbii taught me to connect community activity with a concrete purpose. Its internship challenge, for example, gave members a way to demonstrate skills while helping the organization identify candidates.

## What Still Needs Testing

Tempest has not launched as an operating organization. The research, marketing plan, proposed budget, and campaign mockups are planning work. They do not establish demand or guarantee that sponsors will buy a package.

The next decisions are selecting the initial title and competition level, understanding roster costs, and testing the audience and sponsor proposition. Those decisions will determine how much of the plan is practical with the resources available.

## Why I Am Documenting It

Tempest gives me a way to apply what I am learning about entertainment business to esports operations. I want the portfolio to show the reasoning behind the proposal, the work completed, and the assumptions I still need to test.
