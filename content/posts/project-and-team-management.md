Before this course, I knew that projects needed planning, but I did not think about how many connected decisions keep a project under control. Through PLOM, I learned that planning, leading, organizing, and monitoring have to support one another. I used the project management life cycle to move from initiation and planning through execution, monitoring, and closeout.

When I built the ManaLink plan, I had to connect the scope, schedule, budget, risks, roles, and deliverables instead of treating them as separate assignments. The hardest part was keeping the details consistent across every document because one change to a task, date, or cost could affect the rest of the plan. That process helped me see how project managers keep work organized from the original idea through the final review.

The team performance work also made me examine how I operate with other people. I tend to be reserved, calm, and logical, which can help when a team needs someone to study a problem and make a decision without overreacting. Communicating too little can leave teammates unsure about my progress, and impatience can make me move forward before everyone has the information they need.

I can improve by giving short status updates, assigning clear responsibilities, and checking in before important decisions. While completing the Team Performance Improvement Plan, I learned to use feedback and accountability throughout the project instead of waiting for something to go wrong. I do not need to change my personality, but I do need to make sure the team has the information it needs from me.

Time was the project constraint that concerned me most because the tournament has a fixed event date. Delays in promotion, registration, testing, or bracket preparation could create problems for every task that follows. I also learned that meeting the schedule does not matter if the final experience has poor quality.

I used the Quality Management Plan and the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle to set standards, complete the work, inspect the results, and correct problems. For ManaLink, I can use those steps to check registration accuracy, tournament rules, communication, bracket performance, and the participant experience before the event begins.

My main GPS Map takeaway was that my identity does not depend on a job title or what I own. My analytical and independent personality can support problem solving, but I still need to manage the weaknesses that come with it. I use purpose and passion to decide why a project matters, then I use a plan to turn that interest into specific work. Problem solving helps me respond when reality does not match the plan, and profit means creating enough value and financial support for the project to continue.

I plan to use the completed PM Plan and GPS Map to finish ManaLink and guide future entertainment or business projects with clearer goals, earlier communication, defined risks, and regular quality checks.
