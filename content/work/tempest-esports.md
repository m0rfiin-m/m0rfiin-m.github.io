## Final Business Plan and Presentation

**Updated October 22, 2026 · Full Sail University · Final Project: Business Plan**

### Project Brief and My Role

I developed Tempest as an academic business proposal and organized the research, marketing approach, operating assumptions, and financial outlook into a presentation. The final concept focuses on a proposed Los Angeles VALORANT organization supported by community activity, sponsorships, and merchandise.

My work included structuring the business story, connecting the sections of the plan, and preparing the deck and narration script. The completed presentation materials include 12 slides and a corresponding script covering the market opportunity, company concept, target audience, competition, operations, marketing, milestones, financial outlook, funding assumptions, and ownership.

### How the Plan Developed

The earlier marketing work below explored several FPS titles and a broader campaign budget. The final presentation narrowed the concept to VALORANT and used a proposed first-year marketing budget of $3,600. It also introduced staged paid-player staffing and a scoped sponsor pilot with defined content, community sessions, and reporting.

These revisions made the proposal more specific about what would need to be delivered and funded. I kept projected sales, assumed funding, and proposed launch milestones separate from achieved results. The final materials identify demand testing and funding confirmation as prerequisites to operating the business.

## The Concept

Tempest is an FPS-focused esports organization built around competitive first-person shooter titles: Call of Duty, Valorant, Apex Legends, and Counter-Strike. The proposed organization would be based in Los Angeles and operate remotely. Selecting an initial title and competition level would come before recruiting a roster.

The proposed revenue streams are sponsorship packages and branded merchandise. Team content would support audience growth and sponsor exposure. Proposed digital properties include TempestEsports.gg and TempestEsportsHQ.com.


## Competitive Analysis

I analyzed three major competitors to identify where Tempest fits and how to position against them.

**100 Thieves** informed my analysis of how lifestyle branding, merchandise, and content can support an esports organization. For Tempest, I chose a narrower initial focus on FPS competition.

**FaZe Clan** informed my thinking about creator reach and the risks of tying the organization’s identity too closely to individual personalities.

**Team Liquid** provided a reference for competitive scale and an established fan community. Tempest’s proposed starting point would be smaller, with one initial roster.

Tempest's position: blend competition with content, avoid overreliance on personalities, and stay tightly focused on FPS identity. The intent is to keep early operations manageable and make the audience proposition clear to prospective sponsors.


## Target Market

**Primary audience (ages 16-34):** Competitive FPS players and esports viewers. Core games: Call of Duty, Valorant, Apex Legends, Counter-Strike.

**Secondary audience (ages 18-30):** Gaming lifestyle consumers. Core platforms: Twitch, YouTube, Discord, TikTok, X, and Instagram.

SEO strategy targets phrases including: Tempest Esports Valorant team, tournament recap, community events, FPS esports team for sponsors, and esports merch for FPS fans.

## Content Strategy

The content plan runs on four pillars:

**Competition:** Match highlights, top plays, stat graphics, tournament announcements.

**Personality:** Player spotlights, day-in-the-life content, behind-the-scenes footage, casual team interactions.

**Community:** Polls, Discord discussions, user-generated content features, giveaways and fan reactions.

**Credibility:** Blog articles, press releases, partnership announcements, founder and coach insights.

Every piece of content includes a clear call to action. Formats span short-form video, livestream clips, static graphics, blog posts, email prompts, and posters.

## Proposed Year 1 Marketing Budget: $15,000

| Category | Allocation | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Social Media Ads | 40% | $6,000 |
| Freelance Designer | 30% | $4,500 |
| Networking & Events | 17% | $2,500 |
| Website & SEO Tools | 6% | $900 |
| Email & CRM | 4% | $600 |
| Print Materials | 3% | $500 |

The $15,000 budget is a planning assumption. Spending would depend on available funding and demand for sponsorship packages and merchandise. Revenue forecasts still need to be validated against package prices, sales volumes, and costs. Allocations are rounded.

## Proposed Year 1 Targets

- **Social Media:** 10,000 combined followers across TikTok, Instagram, X, and Twitch with 5% engagement rate by end of Year 1
- **SEO / Website:** 2,500 unique monthly visitors with under 40% bounce rate by Q4
- **Traditional Marketing:** 500 QR code scans from printed materials driving traffic to Discord and the website

## Analytics Plan

The measurement plan would use Hootsuite for social reach and engagement, Google Analytics for website traffic and content performance, and Bitly for offline-to-online conversion from print materials. Social metrics would be reviewed weekly to compare content approaches. Website and traditional marketing performance would be assessed monthly. These are proposed tools and review intervals, not results from a live campaign.

## Status

Tempest remains a concept-stage graduate project. The completed work includes research, marketing plans, campaign mockups, a business plan, and final presentation materials. Before any launch, the concept would require validated customer demand, confirmed funding, and a feasible operating plan.
